Bleeding that does not slow or stop after several minutes of firm, direct pressure — from an injury, a nosebleed, or any other source — is a medical emergency. So is coughing up a significant amount of blood (more than streaks or specks in mucus) or vomiting blood, which can appear bright red or look like dark coffee grounds.

Large volumes of blood loss, whether external or internal, can lead to shock — a dangerous drop in blood pressure that starves the body's organs of oxygen. Warning signs of shock include a rapid heartbeat, cold and clammy skin, dizziness, confusion, or fainting, and can develop quickly even if the person initially seems alert.

For uncontrolled external bleeding, apply firm, continuous direct pressure with a clean cloth while calling emergency services — do not repeatedly lift the cloth to check, as this disrupts clotting. Coughing up or vomiting a significant amount of blood needs emergency evaluation even if the person otherwise feels okay, since the underlying cause is not something that can be assessed safely at home.
