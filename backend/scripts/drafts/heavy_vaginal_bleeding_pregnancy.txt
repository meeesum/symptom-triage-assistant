Heavy vaginal bleeding during pregnancy — soaking through a pad within an hour, passing large clots, or bleeding accompanied by severe abdominal or pelvic pain — is always a reason for urgent evaluation, regardless of how far along the pregnancy is. Light spotting can sometimes be unrelated to a serious problem, but heavy bleeding cannot be safely assessed at home.

Possible causes range widely by stage of pregnancy and severity, including but not limited to miscarriage, placental complications, or an ectopic pregnancy — some of which are themselves medical emergencies for the pregnant person, independent of the pregnancy's outcome. Dizziness, fainting, a rapid heartbeat, or severe pain alongside the bleeding are additional signs that this needs emergency, not routine, care.

If you are pregnant and experiencing heavy vaginal bleeding, especially with severe abdominal pain, call emergency services or go to the nearest emergency room now rather than waiting for a routine appointment. Bring a note of when the bleeding started and roughly how much blood has been lost, if you can, since this helps guide urgent evaluation.
