Joint pain can come from many causes — overuse, minor injury, arthritis, or a viral illness — and mild joint pain that comes on gradually often improves with rest, over-the-counter pain relief, and avoiding activities that worsen it.

Joint pain accompanied by significant swelling, warmth, redness, or fever can be a sign of an infected joint or an inflammatory flare that needs prompt evaluation, since an infected joint can cause lasting damage if treatment is delayed. Joint pain following a significant injury, or pain severe enough that the joint cannot bear any weight or be moved, should also be evaluated promptly rather than assumed to be minor.

Joint pain that persists for more than a few days without improvement, or that keeps recurring, is worth discussing with a primary care provider even if it isn't severe — ongoing joint pain can sometimes be an early sign of a condition that responds better to treatment when addressed early.
